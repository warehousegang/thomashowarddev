About

I'm Thomas Howard, CTO of Snap eHealth, an engineer, and the founder of BusyNow.

I'm interested in what happens when software, infrastructure, AI, and automation are treated as parts of the same engineering system rather than separate disciplines.

My work spans product engineering, platform architecture, Kubernetes, cloud infrastructure, reliability, developer experience, and increasingly autonomous engineering systems.

The question I'm most interested in right now is how much of an engineering organization can be encoded into software.

Not just using AI to write code. I'm interested in the system around the code: agents, testing, deployment, observability, incident response, infrastructure, policy, and the feedback loops required to operate software safely.

That's the idea behind what I call an Engineering OS.

BusyNow

I'm the founder and engineer behind BusyNow, a production application I'm building and operating on Kubernetes.

BusyNow is also my proving ground for the Engineering OS. Instead of building platform projects that exist only as demos, I want the infrastructure and automation to have an actual job: keep a real product running.

The experiment is whether a very small engineering organization can operate software at a level that historically required a much larger team.

That means making the engineering underneath BusyNow sophisticated while making the experience of operating it increasingly simple.

Agents, automation, platform infrastructure, testing, observability, deployment, policy, and operational knowledge become parts of the system rather than disconnected tools and processes.

As BusyNow grows, I'm interested in discovering where that model works, where it breaks, and which engineering responsibilities stubbornly refuse to become software.
