About

I'm Thomas Howard, a platform engineer interested in what happens when software, infrastructure, and automation are treated as parts of the same system.

A lot of my work revolves around Kubernetes, cloud infrastructure, internal developer platforms, GitOps, reliability, and increasingly, autonomous engineering systems. I'm interested less in infrastructure for its own sake than in building platforms that make complicated engineering systems easier to operate.

BusyNow

I'm the founder and engineer behind BusyNow, a production application I'm building and operating on Kubernetes.

BusyNow is also where I test a lot of the ideas I write about here. Instead of building platform projects that exist only as demos, I want the infrastructure to have an actual job: keep a real product running.

That has made BusyNow the proving ground for what I'm calling my Engineering OS—an experiment in how much of an engineering organization one person can encode into agents, automation, platform infrastructure, policy, and software.

As the product grows, I'm particularly interested in finding where that idea works, where it fails, and which engineering responsibilities stubbornly refuse to become software.

Go

I also really love Go.

I came to the language through platform engineering and Kubernetes, and it clicked with me in a way I didn't expect. I like that Go is small enough to understand, explicit enough to reason about, and powerful without constantly trying to be clever.

More importantly, learning Go is changing how I think about Kubernetes.

I'm increasingly interested in moving beyond operating Kubernetes to building software that extends it: controllers, operators, custom resources, reconciliation loops, and platform APIs.

I'm still learning. That's part of why I write about it.

I don't want this site to present a polished version of engineering where everything worked the first time. I'd rather document the process of understanding these systems deeply enough to build with them.

Writing

This site is where I write about that work: platform engineering, Kubernetes, Go, reliability, AI, Engineering OS, and whatever I learn from operating real systems.

I don't publish on a schedule just to publish. When I've built something interesting, gotten something wrong, changed my mind, or learned something worth explaining, I'll write about it.
