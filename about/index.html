About

I'm Thomas Howard, CTO of Snap eHealth, an engineer, and the founder of BusyNow.

I'm interested in what happens when software, infrastructure, AI, and automation are treated as parts of the same engineering system rather than separate disciplines.

My work spans product engineering, platform architecture, Kubernetes, cloud infrastructure, reliability, developer experience, and increasingly, autonomous engineering systems.

The question I'm most interested in right now is how much of an engineering organization can be encoded into software.

Not just using AI to write code. I'm interested in the system around the code: agents, testing, deployment, observability, incident response, infrastructure, policy, governance, and the feedback loops required to operate software safely.

That's the idea behind what I call an Engineering OS.

BusyNow

I'm the founder and engineer behind BusyNow, a production application I'm building and operating on Kubernetes.

BusyNow is also where I test a lot of the ideas I write about here. Instead of building platform projects that exist only as demos, I want the infrastructure and automation to have an actual job: keep a real product running.

That has made BusyNow the proving ground for the Engineering OS; an experiment in whether a very small engineering organization can operate software at a level that historically required a much larger team.

That means making the engineering underneath BusyNow sophisticated while making the experience of operating it increasingly simple.

As the product grows, I'm particularly interested in finding where that idea works, where it fails, and which engineering responsibilities stubbornly refuse to become software.

Go

I also really love Go.

I came to the language through platform engineering and Kubernetes, and it clicked with me in a way I didn't expect. I like that Go is small enough to understand, explicit enough to reason about, and powerful without constantly trying to be clever.

More importantly, learning Go is changing how I think about Kubernetes.

I'm increasingly interested in moving beyond operating Kubernetes to building software that extends it: controllers, operators, custom resources, reconciliation loops, and platform APIs.

I'm still learning. That's part of why I write about it.

Becoming a CTO hasn't made me less interested in the engineering. If anything, it's made me more interested in understanding how the pieces fit together; from a reconciliation loop in Kubernetes all the way up to how an engineering organization operates.

Writing

This site is where I write about engineering leadership, platform engineering, Kubernetes, Go, reliability, AI, governance, Engineering OS, and what I learn from building and operating real systems.

I don't want this site to present a polished version of engineering where every architecture decision was obvious and everything worked the first time. I'd rather document the building, the mistakes, the things I change my mind about, and the ideas that survive contact with production.

I don't publish on a schedule just to publish. When I've built something interesting, gotten something wrong, changed my mind, or learned something worth explaining, I'll write about it.
