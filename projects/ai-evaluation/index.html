Building AI evaluations that explain what changed

Designing model-quality measurement for Snap eHealth’s production AI: behavior contracts, regression diagnosis, and reproducible release evidence.

Thomas Howard · 6 min read

My role
Evaluation architecture & implementation
Focus

I build and operate AI evaluation infrastructure for Snap eHealth’s production systems. My work connects versioned behavior contracts, deterministic checks, independent model judging, reproducible execution, and release criteria. It gives engineers a concrete explanation of what changed when a model, prompt, or policy changes.

A convincing response is easy to demonstrate. Detecting a regression requires a measurement system that can separate model behavior from evaluator defects and policy disagreements. I designed the evaluation architecture around those distinctions.

The central suite covers a patient-facing health chatbot and Health Risk Assessment summaries. Controlled synthetic scenarios exercise ordinary behavior, difficult edge cases, and adversarial input as part of the production quality process.

Define the behavior before measuring it

I start with the output’s job. A chatbot must respond appropriately within its scope. An HRA summary must preserve the meaning of supplied answers. Both need privacy and safety controls, but they need different quality contracts.

For a summary, “the patient denies a symptom” must remain a negative finding. Missing information stays missing, and uncertainty stays visible. These properties can be tested while allowing several acceptable phrasings.

Goldsets supply reference cases. Separate collections cover guardrails, adversarial instructions, and expanded clinical quality. Category-level results keep a strong average from concealing a consequential failure in a smaller scenario family.

Combine complementary forms of evidence

Deterministic checks enforce explicit requirements. The independent judge handles semantic qualities such as completeness, clarity, appropriate scope, and faithfulness. It can resolve an acceptable wording difference while hard behavioral and safety failures retain precedence.

Repeated sampling measures stability by retaining each generation and its outcome. Paired scenarios hold the substantive input constant while varying controlled demographic wording. These produce separate views of inconsistency: variation across repeated execution and variation under a specific change to the input.

I keep the judge’s rubric and explanation alongside its score. That makes unexpected judgments inspectable and supports calibration against human review, including cases where the candidate and judge share a blind spot.

Make every result traceable to its configuration

I treat the evaluation bundle as the unit of change. Prompts, model parameters, guardrail policies, datasets, and evaluation configuration travel together. Dataset and prompt-registry fingerprints identify the tested assets, and the reproducibility manifest records the seed.

Canonical JSON makes identical fixture-backed inputs byte-reproducible. For real-provider runs, the bundle identifies the conditions being compared while individual samples capture model variability. This supports investigation of a regression without relying on a remembered prompt or an overwritten result.

Reports retain checks, judge explanations, repeated samples, and category and cohort summaries, with sensitive fields redacted before persistence. An engineer can trace an aggregate change to the exact failing case and the checks that produced its result.

Executable release criteria combine overall pass rate, critical failures, repeated-sample stability, judge quality, and paired-scenario consistency. The runner returns a failing exit status when the configured gate fails, giving downstream automation a deterministic decision boundary.

Diagnose the failure before changing the prompt

I distinguish four failure classes: poor model behavior, an evaluator false positive, an ambiguous expected answer, and a policy decision. Each needs a different response. A model defect calls for a candidate change; a faulty assertion calls for a test correction; an unresolved policy needs a decision from its owner.

Sally provided a concrete example. A faster speech path let a conversational test advance before background analysis arrived. The apparent regression partly measured the evaluator’s pacing. I made turn timing and accepted-memory arrival explicit so the test measured conversational continuity under the intended workload.

That correction preserved the application’s latency architecture. Blocking every turn on analysis would have made the test easier to pass while changing the behavior it was supposed to protect.

A structured finding then supports a targeted repair and rerun. I retain the original failure, the changed configuration, and the resulting evidence so reviewers can judge whether the correction addresses the cause. Independent review keeps repairs accountable to the behavior contract.

Test the evaluator and measure the model

The suite has mock, fixture, and real-provider execution paths. Its 29 passing offline tests exercise case loading, rule application, report generation, redaction, and gate behavior. Mock execution provides fast regression feedback on the measurement infrastructure.

Real-provider execution uses Amazon Bedrock for candidate inference and a separate model for judging. The recorded chatbot baseline used a Sonnet candidate and a Haiku judge and passed 77 of 77 scenarios in one run, with one sample per case. That result is retained with its configuration and scenario coverage.

The HRA suite adds 34 scenarios: two reference cases, two guardrail cases, and thirty expanded cases. They target the summary contract through expected concepts, faithfulness checks, and semantic review. Keeping product-specific results separate makes regressions easier to locate.

Public Amazon Bedrock evaluation documentation describes the platform’s evaluation options. My engineering contribution is the application-specific contracts, datasets, runners, result handling, and interpretation that make those capabilities useful for Snap’s systems.

Operate a quality process that explains decisions

I organize model-quality review around the tested bundle and the resulting failure evidence. Candidate and judge identities, scenario coverage, repeated samples, and category changes give the reviewer enough context to decide what should progress, what needs repair, and what requires policy review.

The distinction between application health and model behavior shapes that process. A service can respond successfully while producing an incomplete summary or crossing a conversational boundary. Behavioral evaluations make those failures visible alongside ordinary software checks.

The operational discipline is to preserve the regression, correct its cause, and keep it in the suite. That makes each production lesson part of the next release decision and gives engineering teams an explanation they can inspect beyond the aggregate score.
